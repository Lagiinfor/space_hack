This is the object's spectrum: its brightness at each infrared colour. Each dot is one SPHEREx photo; each line is one sky pass. Dips in the shaded strips mean molecules (methane, water ice, CO₂, CO) are absorbing light. If the lines don't match, the object changed.

💡 Computer's picks

The computer tracks every dot across the sky passes and points out small areas it finds interesting: things that moved (checked against ESA's Gaia catalogue: known = Gaia predicts it, unexplained = worth a closer look), things that changed brightness, and unusually red objects. They are hints, not verdicts: your eyes decide.

☄ Asteroids and comets

Known objects come from the IMCCE SkyBoT service, which predicts where every catalogued asteroid and comet was at the moment of each photo. "Caught" means that single SPHEREx photo shows extra light right where the object was predicted. Risk information is NASA/JPL's own published data (Small-Body Database, Sentry impact monitoring, close-approach list); this tool does not calculate any risk itself. A few SPHEREx photos cannot pin down an orbit, so for an unknown moving object the right step is to report it to the Minor Planet Center, not to guess.
